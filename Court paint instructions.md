## Correct FIBA marking plan

Use the inner edge of the baseline as `y = 0`. Measure into the court toward the opposite endline. Use the centre of the court as `x = 0`; left is negative and right is positive.

All painted lines are **5 cm wide**.

---

## 1. Establish the court and basket references

### Court

The playing court is:

- Width: **15.00 m**
- Length: **11.00 m**
- Dimensions measured from the **inner edges** of the boundary lines

The boundary lines are outside the playable 15 × 11 m rectangle because they are not part of the court.

Check the rectangle by measuring both diagonals:

- Expected diagonal: **18.601 m**

Do not begin internal markings until both diagonals are equal.

### Court centreline

Mark the midpoint of the baseline:

- 7.50 m from either sideline

Draw a temporary centreline perpendicular to the baseline.

### Basket reference point

The exact point on the floor under the centre of the basket must be:

- On the centreline
- **1.575 m from the inner edge of the baseline**

Use a plumb line from the centre of the rim where possible. Call this point **B**.

This point controls both curved lines:

- 2-point arc
- No-charge semicircle

---

## 2. Mark the restricted area

The restricted area is a rectangle:

- Width: **4.90 m**
- Length: **5.80 m**

### Lane side lines

From the baseline midpoint, measure:

- 2.45 m left
- 2.45 m right

These positions are the **outer edges** of the lane lines.

The resulting outside-to-outside width is:

`2.45 + 2.45 = 4.90 m`

Draw both lane lines perpendicular to the baseline, extending to the free-throw-line level.

Because the lane lines are part of the restricted area, the 5 cm paint should extend inward:

- Left lane line: from `x = -2.45 m` inward toward the centre
- Right lane line: from `x = +2.45 m` inward toward the centre

Each line terminates at the outer/furthest edge of the extended free-throw-line level:

- `y = 5.80 m`

---

## 3. Mark the free-throw line

The actual free-throw line is:

- **3.60 m long**
- **5 cm wide**
- Centred on the centreline
- Parallel to the baseline
- Its edge furthest from the baseline is exactly **5.80 m** from the inner baseline edge

Therefore:

- Left end: `x = -1.80 m`
- Right end: `x = +1.80 m`
- Front/furthest edge: `y = 5.80 m`
- Rear edge, toward the basket: `y = 5.75 m`

Relationship to the restricted area:

```text
Lane edge       Free-throw line                     Lane edge
    |----0.65 m----|========== 3.60 m ==========|----0.65 m----|
    <----------------------- 4.90 m ---------------------------->
```

### Important painting decision

Paint the **3.60 m white free-throw line**.

Do not automatically add another separate 4.90 m white horizontal stripe. The 4.90 m dimension identifies the restricted-area width and the conceptual “extended free-throw line.” If the restricted area is made from darker tiles, its 4.90 m top boundary will already be visually apparent.

---

## 4. Mark the free-throw rebound places

Diagram 2 gives this distance chain along each lane line, starting from the baseline:

1. **1.75 m**
2. Then **0.85 m**
3. Then a **0.40 m neutral zone**
4. Then **0.85 m**
5. Then another **0.85 m**

This creates the following reference positions:

- `y = 1.75 m`
- `y = 2.60 m`
- Neutral-zone section from `y = 2.60 m` to `y = 3.00 m`
- `y = 3.85 m`
- `y = 4.70 m`

Diagram 2 visually shows:

- A short mark at 1.75 m
- The 0.40 m neutral-zone marking
- A short mark at 3.85 m
- A short mark at 4.70 m

Apply these symmetrically outside both lane lines.

The uploaded pages do not numerically state how far the short marks project outward from the lane. A conventional 10 cm projection can be used, but that particular 10 cm dimension is not specified on pages 6–7.

---

## 5. Mark the 2-point line

The 2-point line consists of:

- Two straight lines perpendicular to the baseline
- One circular arc joining them

### Straight sections

Their **outer edges** are:

- 0.90 m from the inner edge of the left sideline
- 0.90 m from the inner edge of the right sideline

Since the court is 15 m wide, their positions measured from the centreline are:

- Left outer edge: `x = -6.60 m`
- Right outer edge: `x = +6.60 m`

The 5 cm line extends toward the basket side:

- Left line extends toward the centre
- Right line extends toward the centre

### Arc

Use basket point B as the centre.

The rules measure the radius to the **outer edge** of the arc:

- Outer radius: **6.75 m**
- Inner radius: **6.70 m**

Paint between those two arcs.

Use a rigid trammel, steel tape or non-stretch cable. Do not use ordinary rope because it may stretch.

### Useful derived checks

The arc meets the straight sections approximately:

- **2.990 m from the baseline**

The highest point of the outer arc is:

- `1.575 + 6.750 = 8.325 m` from the baseline

These are derived checks, not separately printed dimensions in the rules.

---

## 6. Mark the no-charge semicircle

Again use basket point B as the centre.

### Semicircle

The rules measure its radius to the **inner edge** of the line:

- Inner radius: **1.25 m**
- Outer radius: **1.30 m**

Draw only the half facing the open playing court.

The front-most inner edge should therefore be:

- `1.575 + 1.25 = 2.825 m` from the baseline

### Straight side extensions

The semicircle joins two short lines perpendicular to the baseline.

Their inner edges are:

- 1.25 m left of B
- 1.25 m right of B

So:

- Left inner edge: `x = -1.25 m`
- Right inner edge: `x = +1.25 m`

Each straight section is:

- **0.375 m long**
- Running from `y = 1.20 m` to `y = 1.575 m`

This works because:

`1.575 − 1.20 = 0.375 m`

The paint extends outward, away from the centre of the semicircle.

---

## 7. Dry-layout sequence before painting

Do the complete layout first with chalk or removable marking pencil.

Recommended order:

1. Inner edge of the baseline
2. Court centreline
3. Basket point B
4. Court rectangle
5. Lane side lines
6. 5.80 m free-throw reference
7. Centred 3.60 m free-throw line
8. Rebound-place markings
9. 2-point straight sections
10. 6.75/6.70 m 2-point arcs
11. 1.25/1.30 m no-charge semicircle
12. No-charge straight extensions

Have a second person independently verify every measurement before applying tape.

---

## 8. Masking and painting order

For modular tiles:

1. Make sure all tiles are locked and in their final positions.
2. Number the tile rows and columns underneath.
3. Clean and prepare only the areas receiving paint.
4. Use flexible fine-line masking tape for curves.
5. Mask both sides of every 5 cm line.
6. Paint straight lines first.
7. Paint the 2-point arc.
8. Paint the no-charge semicircle.
9. Paint rebound markings last.
10. Remove tape while the final coat is still slightly tacky.
11. After curing, cut the paint film carefully along every tile joint before dismantling the floor.
